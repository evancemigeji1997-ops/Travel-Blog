Paris, France — The Eiffel Tower

Built by engineer Gustave Eiffel for the 1889 World's Fair, the Eiffel Tower stands over 300 meters tall and was once the tallest man-made structure on Earth (Encyclopædia Britannica, 2026). Today it is the symbol of Paris, drawing millions of visitors who climb or take a lift to its observation decks for sweeping views over the Seine and the city's rooftops.

Rome, Italy — The Colosseum

Completed around 80 AD, the Colosseum was the largest amphitheater ever built in the Roman Empire, seating tens of thousands of spectators for gladiator contests and public spectacles (Britannica, 2026). Its ring of arches has survived nearly two thousand years and remains one of the most recognizable landmarks of the ancient world.

New York City, USA — The Statue of Liberty

A gift from France dedicated in 1886, the Statue of Liberty stands on Liberty Island in New York Harbor holding a torch and a tablet inscribed with the date of American independence (PathCulture, n.d.). It has long welcomed travelers arriving by sea and remains a lasting symbol of freedom and opportunity.
